ABOUT GANDHI

A Quiet Life That Changed History

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, India. Trained as a lawyer in London, he later spent more than two decades in South Africa, where experiences of racial discrimination deeply shaped his understanding of justice, dignity and resistance.

Gandhi developed Satyagraha, a philosophy centered on truth and nonviolent resistance. Rather than answering injustice with violence, he believed that disciplined civil resistance could expose injustice and awaken the conscience of both the oppressor and the oppressed.

After returning to India in 1915, Gandhi became one of the central figures of the Indian independence movement. He encouraged ordinary people to participate through boycotts, marches, civil disobedience and community action.

His influence reached far beyond India's independence struggle. His commitment to nonviolence, simplicity and moral courage later inspired leaders and movements around the world.

Early Life & Formation

PORTRAIT
1869

From Porbandar to London

Gandhi grew up in a Hindu family in Gujarat. His childhood was influenced by religious traditions, ethical discipline and stories emphasizing truth and compassion.

In 1888, he travelled to London to study law. The experience broadened his worldview while strengthening his interest in personal discipline and moral responsibility.
